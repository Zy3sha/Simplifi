# OBubba Diamond-Level Marketing Strategy

## The Big Idea

De Beers did not sell carbon. It made a diamond feel like the physical proof of forever.

OBubba should not sell tracking. It should make a shared baby brain feel like the modern proof of calm, loving care.

Core line:

**Your baby does not come with a manual. OBubba becomes the memory.**

Sharper performance line:

**Every nap chart was wrong for my baby. OBubba started learning her rhythm.**

Category idea:

**The Shared Baby Brain**

This is bigger than a feature claim. It reframes the category from "baby tracker" to "the thing your family relies on so one exhausted parent does not have to hold the whole baby routine in their head."

## What Competitors Are Really Selling

Huckleberry:
- Owns sleep expertise, SweetSpot, expert guidance, and scale.
- Threat: parents already associate Huckleberry with sleep prediction.
- Weakness to exploit: prediction can feel like another rule or chart when baby does something different.

Napper:
- Owns tired-parent sleep relief and natural rhythm language.
- Threat: emotionally clear for parents who only care about sleep.
- Weakness to exploit: narrower mental territory if parents need whole-family tracking and handover.

Nara:
- Owns calm, free, ad-free tracking across baby, pregnancy, postpartum, and caregivers.
- Threat: high trust and broad tracking.
- Weakness to exploit: less forceful "what should I do next?" positioning.

Onoco:
- Owns family/caregiver sharing and broad routine planning.
- Threat: strong on care-team coordination.
- Weakness to exploit: more operational than emotionally iconic.

OBubba:
- Should own the emotional + practical bridge:
  **It learns your baby's rhythm and shares that rhythm with everyone who cares for them.**

## The University-Level Tactic

Make parents feel slightly absurd for trying to run baby care from memory, WhatsApp, notes, and generic wake-window charts.

The cultural shift:

Before:
"I track baby stuff when I remember."

After:
"Of course we use OBubba. It is our baby's shared brain."

That is the De Beers-style move. The product becomes attached to an identity:

- Good partners stay synced.
- Calm grandparents know the routine.
- Tired parents do not guess alone.
- A baby's rhythm is personal, not generic.

## Brand Platform

Primary promise:

**OBubba learns your baby's rhythm, then keeps the whole family in sync.**

Emotional promise:

**Less guessing. Less asking. Less carrying it all alone.**

Enemy:

Generic charts, mental load, forgotten logs, one-parent memory, and "when did she last feed?" texts.

Hero:

The tired but loving parent who is trying to do everything right.

Role of OBubba:

The gentle brain beside the family. It remembers, learns, and shares.

## Campaign Territories

### 1. The Chart Was Wrong

Purpose: steal sleep-app attention without sounding like a sleep-app clone.

Hooks:
- Every nap chart was wrong for my baby.
- The app said nap. My baby said absolutely not.
- Wake windows helped until my baby ignored them.
- I did not need another average. I needed my baby's pattern.

Payoff:
- OBubba learns feeds, naps, wake windows, and bedtime rhythm from your actual logs.

### 2. The Shared Baby Brain

Purpose: own the cross-platform family sync wedge.

Hooks:
- Mum logs it. Dad sees it. Nobody has to ask.
- Stop making one parent remember everything.
- The baby routine should not live in one tired brain.
- iPhone parent. Android parent. One baby brain.

Payoff:
- Feeds, naps, nappies, sleep, and care notes stay synced across the people helping.

### 3. The 3am Rescue

Purpose: make the app feel necessary at the highest-emotion moment.

Hooks:
- Baby crying. I could not remember what came last.
- At 3am, I do not need another chart.
- Last feed? Long wake? Nappy? What helped?
- The tired version of you deserves backup.

Payoff:
- OBubba keeps the timeline and points you toward the likely next step.

### 4. The Founder Truth

Purpose: build trust and turn OBubba from software into a mission.

Hooks:
- I built OBubba because baby care was living in my head.
- Every app tracked things. I wanted one that helped me understand.
- My baby did not follow the chart, so I built something that learned her rhythm.

Payoff:
- Real-parent founder story, then app UI as proof.

### 5. The Grandparent/Nursery Handover

Purpose: own care coordination beyond the two-parent household.

Hooks:
- Grandma should not need five texts to know the routine.
- Nursery handover should not be a memory test.
- Everyone helping baby should see the same picture.

Payoff:
- OBubba turns the day into a simple shared care guide.

## Measurement

Do not optimize only for installs.

The ad promise is only true if parents reach the "oh, this helps" moment quickly.

Track:
- install
- child added
- first log
- second log
- first prediction viewed
- partner/carer invited
- day-3 return
- trial started

Best early quality signal:

**Cost per first log**

Best retention signal:

**Cost per child added + day-3 return**

## Notes From Research

- Huckleberry's public App Store positioning emphasizes SweetSpot, expert sleep guidance, AI guidance, and all-in-one tracking.
- Napper leans hard into natural rhythm, tailor-made sleep schedules, sleep pressure, and tired-parent relief.
- Nara is broad, calm, free/ad-free, and strong on tracking across baby, pregnancy, postpartum, and caregivers.
- Onoco is strong on caregiver sharing, routine planning, AI nap scheduling, and all-inclusive family access.
- TikTok's own creative guidance stresses hooks early in the video.
- Google App Campaign guidance recommends creative assets grouped around distinct user needs and single selling points.
- Apple custom product pages can align ad creative and store pages for different keyword/audience themes.
