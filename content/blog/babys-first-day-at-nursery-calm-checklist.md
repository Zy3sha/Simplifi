Your baby's first nursery day is nearly here, and the bag is packed. What actually matters before you walk through the door?

**Agree a settling plan with the nursery, give one clear care handover, and decide how you will say goodbye and get an update.** You do not need to make your baby follow a perfect nursery timetable in advance. A familiar adult who knows their feeding, sleep, comfort and health needs is more useful than a rehearsed schedule.

This is general information for families using childcare in the UK; your setting's policies and your baby's individual medical advice come first.

## Before the first day: ask four questions

If your nursery offers settling-in visits, use them to meet the key person and let your baby explore with you nearby. An [NHS-run nursery's settling guidance](https://nurseries.berkshirehealthcare.nhs.uk/parent-hub/settling-in-transitions) describes short visits and a gradual move to longer sessions, tailored to the child. Not every setting offers the same arrangement, so ask what is possible rather than assuming a fixed number of visits.

1. **Who is my baby's key person, and how will we exchange updates?** Ask when you can call, what the nursery reports at pickup, and whether it uses its own paper or digital records.
2. **How will feeds work?** Tell staff how your baby currently feeds, their usual cues and any difficulty with a bottle. Agree what milk and equipment you supply, how it will be labelled, and the setting's preparation and storage process. Follow the [NHS guidance for expressed milk](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/breastfeeding/expressing-breast-milk/) or [making up formula](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/bottle-feeding/making-up-baby-formula/) rather than improvising a travel bottle.
3. **Where and how will my baby sleep?** Ask about the sleep space, supervision and what happens if a nap is short. In England, [current Department for Education safer-sleep guidance](https://help-for-early-years-providers.education.gov.uk/health-and-wellbeing/safer-sleep) says early years providers must use a cot, carrycot, Moses basket or travel cot for babies aged 12 months and under. A comfort toy may help while awake, but it does not belong loose in a young baby's sleep space.
4. **What health information do you need before attendance?** Give the nursery its requested written allergy, medicine and emergency-contact details. If your baby has a diagnosed allergy or prescribed emergency treatment, agree the plan with the setting and your child's clinician before the first day. Do not rely on a note in a tracking app as the nursery's medical plan.

If a bottle is proving difficult, try the [no-pressure bottle guide](/blog/baby-refusing-bottle-gentle-plan.html) rather than turning the last days at home into a feeding battle.

## A two-minute handover at drop-off

Tell the key person what is *different today*, not every detail of the last month: last feed, last sleep, medicine already given, any new symptom, and the one thing that usually helps your baby settle. Confirm the pickup person and phone number. Our [baby-care handover checklist](/blog/baby-care-handover-template-grandparents-nursery.html) gives you a reusable place to start.

## The goodbye can be brief and still be kind

A baby may cry when you leave even after a happy settling visit. The [NHS says separation anxiety is common](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/), especially from around 6 months to 3 years. Tears at the door do not, by themselves, tell you how the rest of the day went.

Try the same simple goodbye each time: a cuddle, “I will come back after nursery,” hand your baby to the agreed adult, then leave as planned. Ask staff when they will update you if your baby remains distressed. You can be sad too; you do not need to be perfectly cheerful for this to work. If the distress is prolonged after you leave or continues for weeks, speak with the nursery and your health visitor rather than trying to solve it alone.

## At pickup: collect three useful facts

Ask **when and roughly how much they fed, when they slept, and what helped them settle**. Those details are enough to choose a gentle evening. A shorter nursery nap might mean an earlier bedtime; it does not mean you must force one. Our [nursery-nap evening guide](/blog/baby-naps-at-nursery-bedtime-plan.html) shows how to make that call without treating one unusual day as a new pattern.

You might get a smiling baby, a cross baby, or a baby who wants to be held all evening. Offer connection, food or milk as usual, and a familiar bedtime. Tomorrow's handover can be shorter because both you and the key person have learned something real about your baby.
