Nap transitions are where a lot of good sleep quietly falls apart. Your baby has been napping happily, then suddenly bedtime is a battle, night wakes creep in, or the early mornings start. Often the cause is not a regression at all: it is a **nap transition**, your baby needing one fewer nap than they are being offered, or one more. Getting the nap count right is one of the biggest levers on the whole day and night, so it is worth doing gently and at the right time.

Here is how each transition tends to look, the signs your baby is ready, and how to move without the mess.

## The golden rule: drop late, not early

Most sleep wobbles at these stages come from dropping a nap **too soon**. A baby pushed to fewer naps before they are ready gets overtired, and overtiredness shows up as harder bedtimes, more night waking and earlier mornings, the very problems people mistake for "needing to drop a nap". When in doubt, hold the extra nap a little longer and shorten it, rather than cutting it. It is almost always easier to trim a nap than to rescue an overtired night.

## The transitions, roughly by age

These are typical ranges, not deadlines. Your baby's own rhythm matters more than the calendar, and our [wake windows by age chart](/blog/wake-windows-by-age.html) shows how the awake stretches stretch out as each nap goes.

### 4 to 3 naps (around 4 to 5 months)
The catnaps of the newborn months start to consolidate. The fourth, late-afternoon nap gets harder to get and starts pushing bedtime too late. **Signs:** the last nap is a fight or won't happen; bedtime is drifting later. **How:** let the day settle into three naps and bring bedtime a touch earlier to cover the lost sleep, rather than forcing a fourth.

### 3 to 2 naps (around 6 to 9 months)
The classic first "real" transition. Three naps stop fitting into the day without bedtime sliding late. **Signs:** the third nap refuses to happen or shoves bedtime past a sensible time; wake windows are clearly lengthening. **How:** move to a morning and an early-afternoon nap, lengthen the wake windows gradually, and use an earlier bedtime on the days the second nap runs short.

### 2 to 1 nap (around 13 to 18 months)
Often the trickiest. For a while your toddler needs one nap on some days and two on others. **Signs:** the morning nap gets longer and steals the afternoon nap, or taking two naps pushes bedtime too late and starts causing early waking. **How:** do not switch overnight. Alternate one-nap and two-nap days based on how the night went and how the morning nap looks, gradually pushing the single nap later toward the early afternoon. An earlier bedtime bridges the tired days.

### 1 to 0 naps (often 3 to 4 years, sometimes later)
The last nap goes when the nap starts wrecking bedtime rather than helping the day. **Signs:** bedtime becomes a long, wired battle on the days a nap happened. **How:** replace the nap with a calm "quiet time" so your child still gets a rest, and bring bedtime meaningfully earlier while they adjust.

## Timing beats method

You will find charts that give exact ages, but two toddlers the same age can be a couple of months apart on any transition, and the same child shifts around growth spurts, teething and [developmental leaps](/blog/4-month-sleep-regression.html). That is why the honest signal is not a number on a chart, it is **your baby's own pattern**: whether the extra nap is genuinely wanted, and whether keeping it is quietly pushing bedtime late.
