"How much should a baby sleep?" is one of the first things every tired parent googles, and the honest answer is: **a range, not a number.** Two healthy babies the same age can need noticeably different amounts of sleep, so the totals below are a guide to sanity-check against, not a target to hit. What matters more than the total is whether the sleep is landing at the *right times*, because timing is what actually fixes the nights.

Here are the typical figures, and how to use them without turning your baby into a spreadsheet.

## Typical total sleep by age (over 24 hours)

These are broad, widely-cited ranges, day naps and night sleep combined. Your baby sitting a little outside them is not automatically a problem. The [NHS notes that babies have their own individual sleep patterns](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/helping-your-baby-to-sleep/).

- **Newborn (0–3 months):** roughly 14–17 hours, spread in short chunks around the clock, with no real day/night pattern yet.
- **3–6 months:** roughly 12–16 hours, night sleep starting to consolidate, usually 3–4 naps.
- **6–12 months:** roughly 12–15 hours, often 2 naps, with longer night stretches.
- **1–2 years:** roughly 11–14 hours, usually dropping to 1 nap.
- **3–5 years:** roughly 10–13 hours, the daytime nap gradually disappearing.

Ranges are wide on purpose. A baby at the lower end who wakes happy and content may simply need less sleep than the chart suggests, and that is fine.

## Why the total is not the whole story

Chasing a total misses the thing that actually drives good sleep: **when** the sleep happens. A baby can log "enough" hours on paper and still have rough nights if the naps are mistimed or bedtime has drifted late. The most common hidden cause of broken nights is not too little total sleep, it is [overtiredness from wake windows that ran long](/blog/overtired-baby-signs-fixes.html), which then fragments the night. So rather than counting hours, it is usually more useful to look at the *timing*: are the naps landing in the right windows, and is bedtime early enough?

Our [wake windows by age chart](/blog/wake-windows-by-age.html) and [flexible sleep schedule guide](/blog/baby-sleep-schedule-guide.html) show how the day tends to shape up at each stage.

## When a number is worth a closer look

Totals are reassurance, not a test, but a few patterns are worth raising with a professional rather than solving with timing alone: a baby who seems persistently far below the range and is unsettled or not feeding or growing well, sudden marked changes in sleep, or any worry about breathing, feeding or development. Trust your instinct and speak to your health visitor or GP if something feels off.

How much should a baby sleep? Somewhere in a wide, age-based range, and your own baby's happy, settled version of "enough" matters more than the midpoint.
