If you are searching for a **free baby sleep app**, you have probably already noticed the catch: most of them are free to *download*, but the part that actually helps your baby sleep, the nap prediction, the bedtime that adapts, the reason your baby is waking, tends to sit behind a subscription. The important thing to know is that "free" means very different things across these apps, and the right question is not "is it free?" but **"is the free tier enough to test whether it fixes my baby's sleep before I pay?"**

## What "free" usually means in a baby sleep app

Across the popular trackers, free tiers fall into three rough buckets:

1. **Free logging only.** You can record feeds, naps and nappies, and see a basic timeline, but the predictions, insights and schedules are locked. Useful as a notebook, but it will not tell you *when* the next nap should be or *why* last night fell apart.
2. **Free with a hard paywall after a trial.** You get the good features for a few days, then they disappear. This is where a lot of the "features hidden behind a paywall" frustration comes from, because you only find out what is paid once you are already relying on it.
3. **A genuinely useful free tier.** Core tracking plus enough of the intelligence that you can actually judge whether the app fits your baby, with premium adding depth rather than unlocking the basics.

The third kind is the one worth your time, because sleep help you cannot test is help you cannot trust.

## What is usually worth paying for

Paid tiers are not automatically a rip-off. The features that genuinely justify a subscription are the ones that take real work to build and keep improving:

- **Nap and bedtime prediction that learns your baby's own rhythm**, not a generic age chart. This is the single biggest lever on sleep, and it is the thing a good engine earns its keep on.
- **Plain-language insight into *why* the night went wrong**, an overlong wake window, a nap that ran short, a bedtime that drifted, so you are fixing the cause, not guessing.
- **Trends, reports and a personalised schedule** that adapt as your baby grows.

What should *not* be paywalled, in our view, is the basic ability to log your baby's day and share it with the other parent. Those are table stakes.

## How to test a free baby sleep app properly

1. **Log for at least 3 to 5 days.** Prediction needs your baby's real data before it gets personal, so a first-day impression is not a fair test.
2. **Check the nap predictions against reality.** Did the suggested wake window and nap time match when your baby actually got tired?
3. **See whether it explains the bad nights**, not just logs them.
4. **Make sure the basics are not crippled.** If you cannot log a full day or share with your partner for free, that is a red flag.
