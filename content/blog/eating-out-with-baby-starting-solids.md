You have booked a table. Your baby has just begun solids. Do you need a cooler, a full meal, a clean highchair and a perfectly timed nap? **No. Keep the outing small:** take one familiar, safely prepared food if you want to offer solids, keep chilled food cold, make sure your baby can sit upright and be watched while eating, and be willing to skip the solids if the setting does not work. Their usual milk feeds still matter much more than one café meal in the early weeks of weaning.

This is general information for a baby who is ready for solids, usually from around six months, not a personal feeding plan. If your baby was premature or has swallowing difficulties, a known food allergy or other medical needs, follow the plan from your health visitor or clinical team.

## Before you leave: make one easy choice

You can feed your baby at home and simply enjoy the outing. When babies first start solids, the [NHS says a small amount once a day is enough](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/what-to-feed-your-baby/from-around-6-months/); breast milk or first infant formula remains their main drink in the first year. A café visit does not have to become a feeding test.

If you do want to offer food while out, choose something your baby has already tried and that you can prepare in an age-appropriate shape and texture. This is not the easiest moment to test a new allergen, especially if you cannot check ingredients or watch your baby comfortably. If a restaurant dish is for the family, ask what is in it and avoid assuming the baby-sized portion is suitable: [NHS guidance advises no added salt or sugar for babies](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/what-to-feed-your-baby/from-around-6-months/). A plain component you can identify may be simpler than a mixed sauce.

## A tiny bag list

- **Food:** one small portion in a clean, closed container. Pack only what you expect to offer; you can always offer more from an untouched portion.
- **Cold storage if needed:** a cool bag with an ice block or frozen water bottle for foods you would normally refrigerate, such as yoghurt, cooked food or a prepared sandwich.
- **Eating basics:** a bib, a clean spoon if you use one, wipes or a cloth, and a cup for sips of water with a meal if your baby is around six months or older.
- **Your usual milk-feed kit:** whatever you normally need for a breastfeed or bottle. Follow the relevant milk-preparation and storage guidance separately; this food checklist is not a formula-storage plan.

The [NHS advice on baby food on the go](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/safe-weaning/storing-and-reheating-food/) recommends keeping chilled food cold with a cool bag and ice blocks. If food that needs chilling has *not* been kept cold, it says to eat it as soon as possible and within four hours at most; throw away leftovers. That is an upper limit, not a target for a warm car or a reason to serve food you are unsure about. If you cannot keep a perishable food safely chilled, choose another option or leave solids for home.

## At the table: check the seat before the menu

Ask whether the venue has a stable highchair with a working harness. Seat your baby alert and upright, fasten the harness securely, and stay with them throughout the meal. The [NHS safer-preparation guide](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/safe-weaning/preparing-food-safely/) says babies should be safely upright in a highchair and supervised while eating. If there is no suitable eating position, do not feed solids in a reclined pushchair or while walking around with food in their mouth. It is fine to wait until you get home.

Look again at each piece of food before offering it. Whole grapes and cherry tomatoes need quartering; firm fruit or vegetables may need softening; remove pips, stones and bones. Size, shape and texture matter even when the food itself is familiar. See our [gagging-versus-choking guide](/blog/gagging-vs-choking-baby-weaning.html) for the difference between a loud gag and a choking emergency, and the [NHS first-aid instructions](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/) for what to do if a child cannot breathe properly. Do not rely on an app during an emergency: call for help and follow first-aid guidance.

Offer a little, then pause. A baby who turns away, closes their mouth or is too tired for the highchair can stop. You can keep your own meal going and try solids another time. If the chair itself is the difficult part, our [calm highchair reset](/blog/baby-hates-highchair-mealtime-reset.html) may help you think it through at home, without an audience.

## Afterwards: keep the record lighter than the day

Throw away food left on your baby's plate or any half-eaten portion of previously prepared food; do not return it to the fridge. The [NHS storage guide](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/safe-weaning/storing-and-reheating-food/) is clear about this. Wash feeding equipment and hands when you can. If you want to remember what happened, a note such as “lunch out: familiar banana, a few tastes, stopped when tired” is enough. One small meal is not a verdict on appetite or progress.

If feeding is regularly difficult, your baby coughs or chokes with food, or you are worried about intake or growth, speak with your health visitor or GP. For signs of a severe allergic reaction or breathing difficulty, seek urgent medical help. An outing should not require you to solve a health concern alone.
