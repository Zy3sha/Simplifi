## Start with the moment when you are most tired

A baby tracker should take work away at 3 a.m., not give you another dashboard to manage.

Start with three questions:

1. Can I log the next feed, sleep or nappy or diaper in a few taps?
2. Can another caregiver understand what happened without a tired handover?
3. Can I look back at the day without filling in everything I missed?

There is no need to recreate the whole day. A useful tracker should still help when you begin with the next real moment.

## Core tracking

- Feeds, including breastfeeding, bottles and mixed feeding.
- Sleep, naps, bedtime and night wakes.
- Wet and dirty nappies or diapers.
- Medicine, temperature and care notes.
- Weaning, allergens and reactions when baby is ready.
- Growth, milestones and memories.

## Sharing and reports

- Partner access or shared context.
- Grandparent, nursery or babysitter handovers.
- Reports for appointments or health visitor conversations.
- Export or summary options that are easy to read.

## Product feel

- Fast logging when you are tired.
- Calm language.
- No shaming around sleep, feeding or routines.
- Useful patterns, not overwhelming charts.
- Privacy that is easy to understand. Use this [eight-question pregnancy and baby app privacy checklist](/blog/pregnancy-baby-app-privacy-checklist.html) to compare accounts, cloud storage, sharing, AI, analytics, location, export and deletion.

## Questions to ask

- Does this app help at 3 a.m.?
- Can another trusted adult understand the day from it?
- Does it keep feeds, sleep and nappies or diapers together?
- Does it still feel useful after the newborn phase?
- Can I use only the parts that help my family?
