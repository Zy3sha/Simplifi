## Step 1: ditch the clock, watch the cues

The first part of your 5-minute routine setup is a mindset shift. Stop treating the clock like the boss and start treating your baby like the source data.

Babies are surprisingly good communicators, even before they can speak. They give us cues: small physical signals that say "I am hungry", "I am tired", or "I have had enough of this sensory-overload toy, thank you very much."

- **Tired cues:** Rubbing eyes, pulling ears, looking away, yawning, becoming unusually still, or the classic thousand-yard stare.
- **Hunger cues:** Rooting, sucking hands, opening and closing their mouth, wriggling, becoming restless or getting fussy.
- **Enough cues:** Turning away, relaxing their hands, slowing down, falling asleep at the breast or bottle, or seeming overwhelmed.

The [NHS responsive feeding guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/feeding-your-baby/bottle-feeding/bottle-feeding-your-baby/feeding-on-demand/) says parents do not need a strict feeding schedule and should follow hunger cues. The [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/infant-and-young-child-feeding) also supports responsive feeding as part of appropriate infant and young child feeding.

When you start tracking these cues in the [OBubba app](https://obubba.com), the pattern becomes easier to see. OBubba does not just store a list of events; it helps you connect them.

## Step 2: use micro-tracking

You do not need to spend all day on your phone. To create a **personalized baby routine** that actually works, you only need to log the parts that shape the day.

1. **Sleep:** When did they drift off, wake up, nap or resist sleep?
2. **Feeding:** Breastfeeding, bottles, expressed milk, pumping notes, top-ups, snacks or full feeds.
3. **Nappies:** Wet and dirty nappies, because they often explain more than we expect.
4. **Mood and notes:** Happy, unsettled, refluxy, gassy, colicky, teething, poorly or just not quite themselves.
5. **Growth and milestones:** Especially helpful for preterm babies, growth spurts and development waves.

It can take around 30 seconds to log a feed or a sleep. Do that for a day or two, and OBubba starts giving you a clearer picture. Wake windows, nap timing, feeds and fussy spells stop feeling like random chaos and start looking like a rhythm.

**Important note:** Always follow your paediatrician, GP, midwife, health visitor or neonatal team's advice for feeding, weight gain, reflux, colic, preterm baby care and any health concerns. OBubba is a tracking and parenting support app, not a medical device.

## Step 3: create a flexi-routine

Once you can see your baby's rhythm, build a flexi-routine. This is not a strict timetable. It is a familiar sequence.

A gentle daytime routine might look like this:

1. Feed.
2. Change.
3. Play or cuddle.
4. Watch for sleepy cues.
5. Wind down.
6. Sleep.

By keeping the order familiar but letting the timing shift based on your baby's cues, you give your baby predictability without turning your day into a military operation.

For example, instead of "nap at 9:15 AM", your routine becomes: "after the first feed and a short awake window, we dim things down when sleepy cues appear." That is much easier to live with.

## Why gentle works

This is not about being vague. It is about being responsive.

Trusted guidance already recognises that babies vary. The [AASM sleep duration guidance](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/childsleepduration.pdf) gives sleep ranges, not exact nap timetables, because sleep needs vary by age and individual biology. The [NHS safer sleep advice](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-basics/newborn-and-baby-sleeping-advice-for-parents/safe-sleep-advice-for-babies/) focuses on a safe sleep environment: back sleeping, a clear cot, a firm flat mattress, and room-sharing for the first 6 months.

A **personalized baby routine** combines both ideas: respond to the baby in front of you, while keeping the non-negotiable safety foundations in place.

That means:

- Feed responsively, not by forcing a hungry baby to wait for a chart.
- Use wake windows as a guide, not a timer with a siren attached.
- Keep sleep spaces safe every time.
- Track enough to understand patterns, not so much that logging becomes another thing to feel guilty about.
